How this works, and its limits

Primary source: YouGov's Westminster MRP model, published 24 Sept 2026 — fieldwork 23 Aug–18 Sept 2026, 16,758 respondents, independent (commissioned by YouGov itself, not any party). Read the write-up · full seat-by-seat data (CSV), including the 95% credible interval (high/low bound) behind every number on this page.

National tactical-vote willingness: YouGov, 23 Sept 2026 — "What is the tactical voting landscape in September 2026?". Labour-vs-Reform head-to-head: Labour 41%, Reform 26%. Conservative-vs-Reform: Conservatives 35%, Reform 20%.

About the Electoral Calculus / Find Out Now poll (24 Sept 2026): that poll was commissioned by Restore Britain — the party this tool excludes from recommendations. Worth knowing before treating its "tactical voting saves Reform 43 seats" framing as neutral. We use the independent YouGov MRP instead.

Cross-check: PollCheck.co.uk runs a continuously-updated swingometer (not a full MRP) against a rolling 7-poll average. It broadly agrees on which seats are close, though the two models disagree on individual seats by a few points — a reminder any single seat's rank can shift.

How "close" is decided: every constituency here shows Reform UK's vote share against the strongest other party — excluding Reform, Restore Britain, and other extreme parties. A seat counts as close when Reform's own plausible upper bound (from YouGov's 95% credible interval) reaches at least as high as the leading alternative's plausible lower bound — i.e. it's statistically plausible the race could go either way, not just that the two headline numbers are near each other. This is deliberately more inclusive than a simple points-based cutoff, because MRP estimates at constituency level carry real uncertainty (typically ±6-10 points). Of 632 GB constituencies, 401 meet this bar. The "gap" column shows the size of that lead either way, as a plain positive number of points.

Coverage: all 632 GB constituencies (Northern Ireland isn't covered by this MRP). Every seat is searchable and listed in the table below, whether or not it's currently close.

Postcode lookup: a published web page like this one can't call outside services live (a platform-level restriction, not a choice), and we don't have a verified, complete postcode-to-constituency dataset we could safely bake in — a wrong match in a voting tool is a real problem, not a cosmetic one. So postcode lookup is a two-step hand-off to Parliament's own official tool rather than one seamless box.

Worth weighing the other side: tactical-voting models are estimates, not certainties. Some people think voting tactically undermines honest representation, or that a party with real, substantial support (Reform is currently polling in the low-to-mid 20s nationally) shouldn't be treated as illegitimate. This tool doesn't take a view on that — it tries to answer "given the current numbers, who's best placed to beat them here" as transparently as possible.

Independent project, not affiliated with any party or pollster. Party colours are used descriptively to identify parties, not as an endorsement by those parties. Data snapshot: 24–25 Sept 2026.
